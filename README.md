# blockchain_benchmark
Distributed ledger technologies have attracted growing interest across enterprise domains owing to their capacity to record transactions in a verifiable and tamper-resistant manner. Among the primary design considerations when adopting such technologies is the choice between open-participation (public) networks and access-controlled (permissioned) networks, each offering fundamentally different trade-offs between transparency and efficiency. This paper presents an empirical benchmarking study contrasting Ethereum, a representative public blockchain platform, with Hyperledger Fabric, a widely deployed permissioned framework, in scenarios representative of supply chain transaction workloads. Experiments were conducted by executing smart-contract-driven transactions on a locally hosted Ethereum network while modelling Fabric's transaction pipeline through a calibrated simulation reflecting its endorsed-order-validate execution model. Four performance dimensions were examined: transactional throughput measured in transactions per second (TPS), per-transaction confirmation delay (latency), workload scalability, and computational resource consumption. Outcomes confirm that the access-controlled architecture delivers markedly superior throughput and reduced latency attributable to its streamlined consensus design, whereas the open network offers stronger decentralisation guarantees at a measurable performance cost. The findings offer practical guidance for architects selecting blockchain platforms for logistics, inventory management, and provenance tracking applications.
